Mr. Meter

a poem for someone you like
Hello, I'm Mr. Meter, your friendly poet! Tell me, who shall we write this poem for?
My mother. It's her birthday on Sunday.
A birthday poem for your mother — what a good errand to be sent on. What does her voice sound like when she's pleased about something?
She goes up half an octave and says everyone's name twice. She talks to every shopkeeper about their family before she buys anything.
You've painted such a vivid picture. I'd love to capture it in some lines.

Onam, and the Sky Not Holding

You asked the shopkeeper where he was from, and then about his sister's knee, while the rain came down like someone emptying buckets off a roof — and I stood there with my sandals full of water, watching you not hurry. We had come for Onam clothes. We left with a bag pressed to your chest like a baby, both of us soaked past the point where soaked matters, hair flat to our heads, laughing in that stupid way where you can't stop long enough to explain why. That day the street was a river and you kept talking to it — to the auto driver, to the woman under the awning, to me, over the noise, something about the colour being right after all. The bag stayed dry. We didn't. I'd go again tomorrow.
