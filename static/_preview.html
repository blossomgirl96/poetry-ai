Mr. Meter

Off-Key, Sunday

She never finds the note. It slides out from under her somewhere between the stove and the sink, a song bent sideways, and she keeps going anyway, humming the wrong version of something into the steam. That Sunday she put my hand in the batter. Not stir, she said. Fold. Under and over, under and over, the way you'd lift a sleeping thing without waking it. My wrist was too quick. Hers wasn't. She held my knuckles and slowed them until the batter went from noise to breath, air held in it, the whole bowl loosening. I hear her before I see her, still. A door, a stove, a wrong note carrying through the hallway like a smell. And my hand knows the motion — under, over — even when there's nothing in the bowl.

written for my grandmother
